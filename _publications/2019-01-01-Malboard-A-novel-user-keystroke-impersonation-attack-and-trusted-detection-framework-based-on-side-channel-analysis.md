## Abstract

Physical hardware implants such as weaponized USB devices present an escalating threat to enterprise security. Traditional keystroke dynamics behavioral biometrics (KDBB) verify legitimate users by measuring flight time and hold time intervals between keystrokes. 

In this paper, we demonstrate **Malboard**, a hardware-implant attack that bypasses modern KDBB systems by dynamically analyzing the legitimate user's typing rhythms and generating synthetic keystroke injection tailored to mirror the user's biometric characteristics. To counter this threat, we develop and evaluate a multi-modal trusted detection architecture leveraging side-channel acoustic emission and USB communication timing to differentiate authentic physical user keystrokes from automated hardware-injected events.
